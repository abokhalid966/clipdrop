How to use ClipDrop

ClipDrop quietly keeps the last moments of your game. When something cool happens, press a key and it's saved. Then watch, edit and send it to your phone.

1. Getting started

  1. Run ClipDrop-Setup.exe once. It sets everything up and adds ClipDrop to your Start Menu (and Desktop if you want). If Windows says "Windows protected your PC", click More info then Run anyway.
  2. ClipDrop lives by the clock (bottom right of the taskbar) as a small orange icon. It may be hidden under the ^ arrow.
  3. Right-click the icon for the menu: Open ClipDrop, Save clip now, Recording on/off, Connect phone, Open clips folder, Help, Quit.
  4. If Windows asks about the firewall, tick Private networks and click Allow. That lets your phone reach your PC on your home Wi-Fi.
ClipDrop never uploads anything. Your clips only travel between your PC and your phone on your own Wi-Fi.

2. Saving clips

While you play, press your hotkey. The default is Ctrl + F9, which saves the last 30 seconds.

  • You hear a short ding and see Clip saved in the top-right corner.
  • Want different lengths? Add more hotkeys in Settings, for example Ctrl + F10 for the last 2 minutes.
  • On your phone, tap the orange button at the bottom (Save last 30 s) to save from there. With several lengths, it asks which one.
  • Clips are saved in Videos\ClipDrop, in a folder named after the game.

The dot at the top of ClipDrop shows what's going on: red = recording, yellow = waiting for a game, grey = off.

3. Your phone

  1. Your phone and PC must be on the same Wi-Fi.
  2. On the PC: right-click the ClipDrop icon → Connect phone. A QR code appears.
  3. Point your iPhone camera at it and tap the link.
  4. In Safari tap Share → Add to Home Screen. Now ClipDrop has its own icon and opens like an app.

Your phone link: __PHONE_URL__

Keep this link private. Anyone on your Wi-Fi with it can see your clips. Android phones work too, except auto-send to Photos.

Saving a clip to your iPhone

Tap Save, then Download. The clip goes into the Files app. To put it in Photos: open Files → Downloads → tap the clip → Share → Save Video. Or set up auto-send below so it happens by itself.

4. Sending files between PC and phone

Open the Files tab at the top of ClipDrop. It works both ways, with photos, videos, documents or any other file.

From your phone to the PC

  • Tap Send to PC and pick photos, videos or files. A bar shows the progress.
  • On the PC they go to Downloads\ClipDrop Shared\From (your phone's name), and a small message pops up.
  • For full-quality videos, set up Send to PC in the share menu. iPhone sometimes shrinks videos picked inside a web page.

From the PC to your phone

  • Drag files into the ClipDrop window, or click Send to phone.
  • Or in Windows: right-click any file → Send to → ClipDrop (send to phone). On Windows 11, click Show more options first.
  • On the phone, open the Files tab and tap Save. With auto-send on, photos and videos go straight into Photos.

Who sent what

Every file has a tag in its corner: your phone's name (like iPhone) or PC. Use the buttons under the search box to show only one side. You can name each phone in Settings → Files and sharing.

5. Auto-send to Photos (iPhone)

New clips can go straight into your Photos app without opening ClipDrop. Set it up once (about 3 minutes):

Open the step-by-step setup →

  • Best option: it runs every time you open the Photos app, so your clips are waiting for you.
  • Each clip is sent only once. Renaming a clip doesn't send it again.
  • To send older clips: tap Select, choose clips, then tap Send to iPhone. They arrive the next time the shortcut runs.

6. Watching and editing

Tap any clip to play it. Below the video:

7. Organizing clips

  • Search by name or game.
  • Sort by newest, oldest, longest, shortest, biggest, smallest, name or game.
  • Filter with the buttons under the search box: all clips, Favorites, or one game.
  • Select lets you pick many clips at once to favorite, send to your iPhone, or delete.
  • The bar at the top shows how much space your clips use and how much is left on the drive.

8. Which video format should I pick?

FormatPick it whenGood to know
H.264You share clips a lot, or aren't sure.Plays everywhere: every phone, WhatsApp, Discord, editors. Biggest files.
HEVCYou mostly watch on your iPhone and want smaller files.Same quality at about half the size, so it also sends faster. Some Windows apps need the free "HEVC Video Extensions".
AV1You have a new graphics card and keep clips on your PC.Smallest files. Needs RTX 40 / RX 7000 / Intel Arc or newer to record, and iPhone 15 Pro or newer to play.

Settings shows which formats your PC can record. "Share size" copies are always H.264, so they play everywhere.

9. Settings

Tap the settings button at the top right (the two sliders). Settings are grouped into sections (Recording, Video, Hotkeys, Sound, Alerts, Clips and games, Files and sharing, Phone, Updates, Language, App). Use the search box to find any option. Every option has a short explanation, and each section has a "Reset" button. The main ones:

  • When to record: always, or only while a game is open.
  • What to record: the whole screen, or only the game window so Discord pop-ups and notifications stay out.
  • Video: format, quality, frames per second and size.
  • Hotkeys: add as many as you like, each with its own length.
  • Sound: pick which speakers and mic to record, and set their volume.
  • When a clip is saved: turn the sound and the in-game message on or off.
  • Updates: ClipDrop checks for new versions once a day. When one is ready, an orange bar appears at the top: tap What's new, then Update now. It closes for a few seconds and opens again, and your settings, clips and files stay. You can also let updates install by themselves.
  • Language: English or Arabic. "Automatic" uses each device's own language.
  • Games: ClipDrop counts any program that fills the whole screen as a game. You can add games it misses (like one you play in a window), or list programs that aren't games.

10. If something doesn't work

My phone can't connect

  • Make sure ClipDrop is running (icon by the clock) and both are on the same Wi-Fi.
  • Allow ClipDrop through the Windows firewall on Private networks: Start → "Allow an app through Windows Firewall" → tick Private for pythonw.
  • If your router restarted, your PC's address may have changed. Right-click the icon → Connect phone and scan the new QR code.

The hotkey does nothing

  • Another app may already use that key. Settings shows a red message under the hotkey if so. Pick a different one.
  • If recording is set to "Only while I'm gaming", ClipDrop only saves when a game is open.

The game isn't detected

  • Games in a window (not fullscreen) aren't detected on their own. Add the game's program name under Settings → Clips and games → Always count these as games.

A file won't send

  • Keep the phone screen on and the page open until the bar finishes. Big videos take a while on Wi-Fi.
  • Check there's free space on the PC (the bar at the top of Clips shows it).

Clips have no sound

  • Check Settings → Sound. Pick the speakers or headset you actually listen with, not "Windows default".

Clips are black

  • Switch "What to record" to Whole screen, and set the game to borderless or windowed mode.

Still stuck?

Right-click the icon → Quit, then open Start → ClipDrop → ClipDrop (show messages). It shows messages explaining what's wrong. The log file is at %LOCALAPPDATA%\ClipDrop\clipdrop.log.
